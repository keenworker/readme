## Part A

**1.** Who was Arachne? *(2 Marks)*

**Answer:** Arachne was a young girl who lived in ancient Greece and was exceptionally talented at weaving. The cloth she wove was as soft as silk, and her multicoloured tapestries were truly works of art that amazed everyone who came to see them.

---

**2.** Who was Goddess Athena? *(2 Marks)*

**Answer:** Athena was the great goddess of wisdom and crafts in ancient Greece. She had invented weaving along with the loom, the spindle, and the shuttle. She was known to be fair yet stern, and her skills as a weaver were considered divine and unmatched by any human.

---

**3.** Why did Arachne believe she was better at weaving than Athena? *(3 Marks)*

**Answer:** Arachne believed she was better at weaving than Athena because she felt that her skill was entirely the result of her own hard work and effort over long years. She refused to accept that her talent was a gift from Athena. She said that no one could weave better than her — not even Athena, who had invented weaving itself — and that Athena had nothing to do with her abilities.

---

**4.** How did Athena react when she heard Arachne's boastful claims? *(3 Marks)*

**Answer:** When Athena heard Arachne's boastful claims, she was angry but also amused by the idea of a human girl claiming to out-weave a goddess. She felt that Arachne had never once thanked her for the gift of weaving. So she disguised herself as an old woman and went to Arachne's cottage to warn her and give her a chance to correct her pride before things went too far.

---

**5.** What did Arachne weave on her tapestry? What does this tell us about her? *(3 Marks)*

**Answer:** Arachne wove a cloth from the finest silk, but her tapestry depicted the gods as angry and foolish, which was very insulting. This tells us that even in her finest work, Arachne could not let go of her arrogance and disrespect. Her tapestry showed that pride had completely blinded her judgment, and she used her skill to mock the very gods instead of showing them any respect.

---

**6.** Why did Athena win the weaving contest? *(3 Marks)*

**Answer:** Athena won the weaving contest because her tapestry was far more beautiful and meaningful than Arachne's. She wove with sunbeams, the sky, the fields, and the clouds, and her tapestry showed the glory and greatness of the gods in a way that seemed to come alive. The audience agreed that while Arachne's work was skillful, Athena's depiction of the sea, the earth, and the gods was truly extraordinary and divine.

---

**7.** How did Athena punish Arachne after the contest? *(3 Marks)*

**Answer:** Athena told Arachne that she must keep her promise and never touch a loom, spindle, or shuttle again. However, since Athena took pity on her and knew that Arachne could never be happy without spinning and weaving, she gave Arachne a new form — that of a spider — so that she could continue to spin and weave without a loom, using threads from her own body instead.

---

**8.** What happened to Arachne at the end of the story? *(3 Marks)*

**Answer:** At the end of the story, Arachne's body shrank to the size of a tiny black dot, her fingers became legs, and she scuttled into the shadows. She had been transformed into the world's very first spider. From that day on, she continued to spin and weave beautiful, transparent webs from her own body, and her delicate spider's web, hanging with drops of dew in the morning light, is described as her greatest masterpiece.

**4.** What is the moral of the story "Arachne: The Weaver"? *(3 Marks)*

**Answer:** The moral of the story is that pride and arrogance always lead to downfall. Arachne was a brilliant weaver, but her excessive pride made her disrespect even a goddess and refuse to acknowledge the gift she had been given. This led to her losing everything she loved most. The story teaches us to be humble and grateful for the talents we have, to give credit where it is truly due, and never to let success make us boastful or disrespectful towards others.

**Answer:** Athena was both fair and kind in her punishment of Arachne. She was fair because Arachne had agreed to the terms of the contest herself and knew the consequences if she lost. Arachne had also been deeply disrespectful and insulting to the gods in her tapestry, which made the punishment fully deserved. However, Athena also showed great kindness and mercy in the way she carried out the punishment. Instead of simply taking away Arachne's ability to weave forever, she transformed her into a spider so that Arachne could continue spinning and weaving for the rest of her life — just in a new and different form. This shows that Athena punished the pride but still cared for the person beneath it, and her punishment was both just and compassionate.

**6.** Write a complete summary of the story "Arachne: The Weaver" in your own words. *(5 Marks)*

**Answer:** Arachne was a young and talented girl who lived in ancient Greece. She was so skilled at weaving that her soft silken cloth and colourful tapestries amazed everyone. However, as her fame grew, so did her pride. She began to boast that her skill was entirely her own and that no one — not even Goddess Athena, who had invented weaving — could weave better than her. When Athena heard this, she disguised herself as an old woman and went to Arachne's cottage to warn her to be grateful and humble. But Arachne refused to listen and boldly challenged Athena to a weaving contest. At that moment, Athena dropped her disguise and revealed herself as the goddess. She accepted the challenge, and the two agreed that whoever lost would give up weaving forever. They both wove all day in front of a large audience. Arachne wove a beautiful but insulting tapestry that depicted the gods as foolish and angry, while Athena wove a magnificent tapestry showing the glory of the gods, the sky, the sea, and the earth. Everyone declared Athena the winner. Arachne was heartbroken and wept bitterly. Taking pity on her, Athena transformed Arachne into the world's first spider so that she could continue to spin and weave — now without a loom, using threads from her own body. The story teaches us that pride and arrogance lead to downfall, and that true talent must always be accompanied by humility and gratitude.
