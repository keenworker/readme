**3.** Do you think being confident and being proud are the same thing? Why or why not? *(3 Marks)*
- **Answer:** No, being confident and being proud are not the same thing. Confidence means believing in your own abilities while still respecting others and being open to the idea of losing or learning. Pride or arrogance means thinking that you are better than everyone else and refusing to give credit to others. Jolie shows healthy confidence, while Juhi shows unhealthy pride. The story of Arachne teaches us the same lesson — that pride leads to downfall, while confidence combined with humility is always a good quality.

### Part A

**1.** Who was Arachne? *(2 Marks)*
- **Answer:** Arachne was a young girl who lived in ancient Greece and was exceptionally talented at weaving. The cloth she wove was as soft as silk, and her multicoloured tapestries were truly works of art that amazed everyone who came to see them.

**2.** Who was Goddess Athena? *(2 Marks)*
- **Answer:** Athena was the great goddess of wisdom and crafts in ancient Greece. She had invented weaving along with the loom, the spindle, and the shuttle. She was known to be fair yet stern, and her skills as a weaver were considered divine and unmatched by any human.

**3.** Why did Arachne believe she was better at weaving than Athena? *(3 Marks)*
- **Answer:** Arachne believed she was better at weaving than Athena because she felt that her skill was entirely the result of her own hard work and effort over long years. She refused to accept that her talent was a gift from Athena. She said that no one could weave better than her — not even Athena, who had invented weaving itself — and that Athena had nothing to do with her abilities.

**4.** How did Athena react when she heard Arachne's boastful claims? *(3 Marks)*
- **Answer:** When Athena heard Arachne's boastful claims, she was angry but also amused by the idea of a human girl claiming to out-weave a goddess. She felt that Arachne had never once thanked her for the gift of weaving. So she disguised herself as an old woman and went to Arachne's cottage to warn her and give her a chance to correct her pride before things went too far.

**5.** What did Arachne weave on her tapestry? What does this tell us about her? *(3 Marks)*
- **Answer:** Arachne wove a cloth from the finest silk, but her tapestry depicted the gods as angry and foolish, which was very insulting. Her tapestry showed that pride had completely blinded her judgment, and she used her skill to mock the very gods instead of showing them any respect.

**6.** Why did Athena win the weaving contest? *(3 Marks)*
- **Answer:** Athena won the weaving contest because her tapestry was far more beautiful and meaningful than Arachne's. The audience agreed that while Arachne's work was skillful, Athena's depiction of the sea, the earth, and the gods was truly extraordinary and divine.

**7.** How did Athena punish Arachne after the contest? *(3 Marks)*
- **Answer:** Athena told Arachne that she must keep her promise and never touch a loom, spindle, or shuttle again. However, since Athena took pity on her and knew that Arachne could never be happy without spinning and weaving, she gave Arachne a new form — that of a spider — so that she could continue to spin and weave without a loom, using threads from her own body instead.

**8.** What happened to Arachne at the end of the story? *(3 Marks)*
- **Answer:** At the end of the story, Arachne's body shrank to the size of a tiny black dot, her fingers became legs, and she scuttled into the shadows. From that day on, she continued to spin and weave beautiful, transparent webs from her own body, and her delicate spider's web, hanging with drops of dew in the morning light, is described as her greatest masterpiece.

**26.** Write a detailed note on the Preamble of the Indian Constitution and explain all its terms. *(6 marks)*
- **Answer:** The Preamble is the introduction to the Constitution of India. It states the vision and objectives of the Constitution and the goals of the Government. It describes India as a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular and Democratic Republic. Sovereign means India is free to govern itself without any external or foreign interference. Indians are free to make their own laws and no foreign country can interfere in Indian decision-making. Socialist means all citizens must enjoy social and economic equality. All are entitled to equal status, opportunities and a decent standard of living, with no discrimination based on caste or community. Secular means all religions are treated equally. There is no state religion, and people are free to propagate and practise any religion of their choice. The government must protect all citizens without any religious discrimination. Democratic means the people have the right to vote and elect representatives to the government. It is a government of the people, for the people and by the people. All citizens above 18 years of age can vote, which is called Universal Adult Franchise. Republic means the head of the government is elected and not hereditary. There is no role of a king, queen or sultan. The government is always answerable to its citizens.

**5.** What are crops? *(1 mark)*
- **Answer:** When plants of cereals, fruits or vegetables are grown on a large scale at one place, they are called crops.

### Part 2: Types of Crops — On the Basis of Season

**6.** What are Rabi crops? Give two examples. *(2 marks)*
- **Answer:** Rabi crops are also called winter crops. They are grown in the winter season from October to March, and the seeds are sown at the onset of the winter season. Wheat, gram and mustard are examples of Rabi crops.

**7.** What are Kharif crops? Give two examples. *(2 marks)*
- **Answer:** Kharif crops are also called monsoon crops. They are grown during the rainy season, with seeds sown at the onset of the rains and harvested at the end of the monsoon season, from October to November. Paddy, maize and cotton are examples of Kharif crops.

**8.** What are the differences between Rabi crops and Kharif crops? *(3 marks)*

| Rabi Crops | Kharif Crops |
|----------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Also called winter crops | Also called monsoon crops |
| Grown in winter season from October to March | Grown during the rainy/monsoon season |
| Seeds sown at the onset of the winter season | Seeds sown at the onset of rains, harvested in October to November |
| Examples: Wheat, gram, mustard | Examples: Paddy, maize, cotton |

### Part 3: Types of Crops — On the Basis of Requirement

**9.** What is a food crop? *(1 mark)*
- **Answer:** A food crop is an agricultural crop that is grown for human consumption. It fulfils the basic food requirement of people.

**10.** What is a cash crop? *(1 mark)*
- **Answer:** A cash crop is an agricultural crop that is grown to earn profit by selling, and not for personal consumption. It has a good demand and a ready market for sale.

**11.** What are the differences between food crops and cash crops? *(3 marks)*

**12.** Write about rice as a food crop. *(3 marks)*

### Part 5: Development of Agriculture

**27.** What is the Green Revolution? *(1 mark)*
- **Answer:** The increase in food grain production with the help of scientific methods is called the Green Revolution.

**28.** Write a short note on the Green Revolution. *(4 marks)*
- **Answer:** In the early 1900s, India had to import food grains to fulfil its requirements. Between 1950 and 1983, food production in India increased three times. This was made possible through scientific knowledge and modern farming methods using better quality seeds, fertilizers and pesticides. Farmers adopted the use of modern machinery like tractors and harvesters. Many agriculture colleges and fertilizer factories were established. The government started providing high quality seeds, fertilizers and pesticides to farmers at very low costs. Better irrigation and soil conservation techniques helped increase productivity, and proper storage arrangements were made for the harvest. This overall increase in food grain production through scientific methods is called the Green Revolution.

**29.** Describe the development of agriculture in India in detail. *(6 marks)*
- **Answer:** In the early 1900s, India had to import food grains to fulfil the needs of its people. However, between 1950 and 1983, food production in India increased three times. This remarkable development was made possible through scientific knowledge and modern methods of farming. Farmers started using better quality seeds, fertilizers and pesticides, which greatly improved agricultural output. The use of modern machinery such as tractors and harvesters replaced older methods and made farming faster and more efficient. Many agriculture colleges were established to train farmers in modern techniques, and several fertilizer factories were set up. The government played a major role by providing high quality seeds, fertilizers and pesticides to farmers at very low costs. Better irrigation facilities and soil conservation techniques also helped to increase the productivity of the land. Proper storage arrangements were made for the harvested crops to prevent wastage. The overall increase in food grain production brought about through these scientific methods is called the Green Revolution. It transformed India from a food-importing country to a nation that can feed its own population and also export agricultural products to the world.
