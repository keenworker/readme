**g.** Write a detailed note on the poem "The Flint" by Christina Rossetti, discussing its content, theme, and message. **(5 marks)**

**Answer:** The poem "The Flint" was written by Christina Georgina Rossetti, a well-known English poet. The poem is divided into two short stanzas, each of which compares precious gemstones with the simple and plain flint stone. In the first stanza, the poet introduces three precious gems — the emerald, which is as green as grass; the ruby, which is as red as blood; and the sapphire, which is as blue as heaven. All three are beautiful, colourful, and admired by people everywhere. But in contrast, the flint is described as simply lying in the mud — unnoticed, dull, and overlooked. In the second stanza, the poet introduces two more precious stones. The diamond is called a brilliant stone that catches the world's desire, and the opal is described as holding a fiery spark, meaning it glows and reflects different colours. But then the poet ends the poem with the most powerful line: "But a flint holds fire." This single line is the heart of the entire poem. While all the other stones are beautiful and desired, none of them can do what the flint does — produce real, actual fire when struck. Fire gives warmth, light, and the means to survive, making the flint the most useful and truly valuable stone of all. The central message of the poem is that we should never judge anything or anyone by their outer appearance. True value lies not in how something looks but in what it can do. Inner strength, hidden talent, and practical usefulness are far more important than outward beauty or popularity.
